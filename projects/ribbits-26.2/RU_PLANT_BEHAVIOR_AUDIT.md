## 2. Scope and exclusions

Audited requested IDs are `dropleaf`, `barley`, `windswept_grass`, `clover`, `grass_sprouts`, `mycotoxic_daisy`, `hyssop`, five independent Lupine IDs (`blue_lupine`, `pink_lupine`, `purple_lupine`, `red_lupine`, `yellow_lupine`), `cattail`, `duckweed`, `tassel`, and `meadow_sage`, all in the `regions_unexplored` namespace. `regions_unexplored:dropleaf_plant` is included as Dropleaf's non-item stem companion. Potted variants are included only where they establish linkage or acquisition behavior.

The exact artifact contains **no** registry, language, model, texture, loot, tag, recipe, feature, or class reference for `regions_unexplored:grass_sprouts` and no `GrassSproutsBlock`. It instead contains separate `regions_unexplored:stone_bud` and `regions_unexplored:medium_grass` blocks. Grass Sprouts is therefore `UNRESOLVED` as an exact-artifact trade target. A later official 0.6 development commit does explicitly prove the historical Medium Grass + Stone Bud merge; that version-bounded lineage evidence is reported separately and is not substituted as exact-JAR behavior.

`regions_unexplored:redstone_bud` is present, separate, and excluded. It is not Grass Sprouts or the requested historical Stone Bud replacement. Silver Birch blocks, items, trees, features, growth, and generation are excluded. A Tassel feature reference inside the `silver_birch_forest` biome is recorded only because it is a Tassel acquisition path; no Silver Birch behavior is audited. Cave Hyssop and other Mycotoxic content are discussed only where they prove isolation or an acquisition relationship.

## 3. Executive summary

- Dropleaf is the most behavior-heavy entry: a luminous, no-collision, **downward-growing** `GrowingPlantHeadBlock`/`GrowingPlantBodyBlock` pair with random growth, bonemeal extension, and a separate no-item body. It is not vanilla Big Dripleaf: it has no tilt, facing, waterlogging, standing, projectile, or redstone-lock state machine.
- Clover is one ground-cover block with facing and `flower_amount=1..4`; repeated placement combines items in one cell, bonemeal fills the cell and then ejects more Clover, and breaking returns the stored amount. One trade can therefore bootstrap effectively unlimited Clover at a bonemeal cost.
- Windswept Grass, Barley, Mycotoxic Daisy, Cattail, Tassel, and Meadow Sage are double-height. Only Dropleaf uses a different companion registry ID. Ordinary double plants use the same ID for upper and lower halves.
- Duckweed is a surface-floating `WaterlilyBlock` derivative placed by `PlaceOnWaterBlockItem`; it occupies air above water/ice, preserves the support fluid below, is replaceable by other items, has no boat-destruction callback, and does not spread.
- Cattail is the only explicitly waterloggable double plant. Its lower half may be dry or waterlogged on dirt/sand/clay/gravel-family supports; initial placement rejects water in the upper cell, although both halves expose `waterlogged` and post-placement upper bucket behavior remains a runtime edge case.
- Barley is **not a crop** in this artifact: it has no age, seeds, random growth, or bonemeal behavior. It is a static tall plant whose sole food loop is smelting or smoking the placeable Barley item into one bread.
- Windswept Grass is decorative and non-growing, but shears return two placeable items from one two-block plant. That creates reliable multiplicative renewability; without shears it can yield only wheat seeds at 12.5%.
- Mycotoxic Daisy has no poison, damage, slow, collision effect, particle callback, stew effect, dye recipe, or alchemical behavior found. Its only unusual environmental trait is block light level 4; “mycotoxic” is otherwise thematic/visual in this artifact.
- Hyssop and all five Lupines are one-block `RuFlowerBlock`/vanilla `FlowerBlock` derivatives, pottable, bee-attractive, and usable in the vanilla suspicious-stew special recipe. Hyssop carries Luck; Lupines carry Saturation. They do not self-bonemeal or spread.
- Tassel has a significant static balance loop: block use while holding bonemeal pops one Tassel and returns `PASS`; the subsequent successful bonemeal item path consumes one bone meal while its growth callback does nothing. Static dispatch therefore predicts one new Tassel per bone meal, indefinitely.
- Meadow Sage is a double plant whose same-ID item is a custom edible `BlockItem`: nutrition 2, saturation modifier 0.15, and a 50% Instant Health effect. It also crafts blue dye.
- From one traded specimen, Dropleaf, Windswept Grass, Clover, and Tassel are renewable by their own block/loot interactions; the other confirmed items are finite by specimen. Hyssop and Lupines can still appear independently through native-biome grass bonemeal until their shared providers are pruned, but that is an initial-acquisition bypass rather than propagation from the traded plant.
- Natural acquisition is predominantly biome → placed feature → configured feature → state provider. Lupines, Hyssop, Tassel, and Meadow Sage also occur in grouped providers; removing an entire group would suppress unrelated plants. Flower-type configured features may also be invoked by grass-bonemeal biome logic, which makes “natural generation only” suppression context-sensitive.
- Direct standard trade results are mechanically viable for every confirmed inventory item. Dropleaf is classified as requiring its companion block/custom handling in the future implementation; Grass Sprouts is unresolved. Barley and Duckweed are direct-safe, while the remaining confirmed items carry explicit renewability, secondary-use, placement, provider-gating, or item-loss caveats. No item was found to require NBT or data components.

## 5. Detailed per-item findings

### Shared interpretation rules

Except where a class or loot table says otherwise, the registered block and item share the listed ID because `BlockRegistry.registerDefaultBlock` passes the exact block to `ItemRegistry.registerDefaultBlockItem`, which constructs a vanilla `BlockItem`. `registerDefaultBlockNoItem` deliberately omits one. All confirmed trade candidates therefore use their ordinary registered item; none contains NBT/data-component requirements. Static vanilla clone-item behavior should return one registered item for every ordinary item-bearing state, including either half of a same-ID double plant and every Clover amount; Meadow Sage resolves the later same-ID `FoodItemWithBlock`. Dropleaf body instead resolves its linked head item. Potted/no-item companions and every ported 26.2 mapping remain runtime-sensitive, so the matrix tests them rather than claiming observed results.

For ordinary `BushBlock`-derived plants, “dirt” resolves through `#minecraft:dirt` plus farmland. In the 1.20.1 baseline that includes dirt, grass block, podzol, coarse dirt, mycelium, rooted dirt, moss block, mud, and muddy mangrove roots. This artifact extends the tag with Alpha grass; Ashen dirt; Chalk, Deepslate, Stone, and Argillite grass; Deepslate Prismoss and Viridescent Nylium; Peat/Silt coarse dirt, podzol, dirt, farmland, grass, and mud; Prismoss; and Viridescent Nylium. Loaded datapacks can extend tags further. No inspected block survival predicate directly tests biome, dimension, temperature, humidity, precipitation, sky access, or light.

No confirmed plant emits redstone power or comparator output or reads redstone power. Unless specifically noted, there are no player/entity/projectile/vehicle/rain/thunder callbacks, particles, status effects, scheduled/random ticks, horizontal spread, or unusual fluid methods. Cross plants have empty collision and can be walked through. Every ordinary block item can be placed by a survival player if the support, fluid, free-space, and build-height predicates pass.

#### `regions_unexplored:dropleaf`

**Confirmed identity.** `RuBlocks.DROPLEAF` is `DropleafBlock extends GrowingPlantHeadBlock`; its ordinary `BlockItem` places the head. `RuBlocks.DROPLEAF_PLANT` is `DropleafPlantBlock extends GrowingPlantBodyBlock`, is registered with no item, and returns Dropleaf as its linked head. The head returns Dropleaf Plant as its linked body. Both constructors pass growth direction `DOWN` and fluid-tick scheduling `false`; the head passes random-growth probability `0.1`. This is a hanging-vine-style head/body pair, **not** vanilla Big Dripleaf or any subclass of it.

The distinction answers the item-specific interaction questions conclusively: there is no `FACING`, `TILT`, or `WATERLOGGED` property, no leaf platform, no entity-standing tilt/lowering, no projectile-triggered tilt, and no redstone locking/reset. The head has only inherited `age=0..25` with registry default `age=0`; ordinary placement then randomizes the placed head to age 0–24. The body has one property-free/default state. Normal placement produces a head below a support. As growth advances downward, the former head becomes `dropleaf_plant` and a new `dropleaf` head appears below it. Only the lowest segment is the head.

Head properties are cyan map color, random ticks, no collision, instant break, Weeping Vines sound, XZ offset, always-postprocess/emissive rendering, and light 14. The body shares the plant/sound/shape properties but neither random-ticks nor emits light. Both outline overrides are a slender `(4,0,4)..(12,16,12)` column with positional offset; collision is empty, entities pass through, and both IDs are tagged climbable. There is no tint provider—the textures and fixed item model are fully colored.

Survival is inherited from the generic growing-plant superclass: a segment is supported by its linked head/body immediately **above**, otherwise the ceiling block needs a sturdy downward face. Soil, biome, moisture, temperature, sky, and light are irrelevant. Growth accepts air only. There is no water state or fluid tick, so it cannot coexist with water as a waterlogged block. Loss of the ceiling or a middle segment causes inherited head/body conversion and invalidation; static analysis predicts that the lowest remaining supported body becomes a head while unsupported lower pieces disappear, but exact update/drop order is a runtime case.

**Growth and renewal.** A newly placed head selects a random age 0–24. While age <25, eligible random ticks attempt downward growth at probability 0.1; an air block below becomes the new head and the previous head becomes body. Random growth ends at age 25, yielding a total hanging column of 2–26 blocks under unobstructed placement depending on initial age. Bonemeal is valid when air exists below; applying it to a body searches down to the head. The inherited Nether-vines count grows at least one and possibly several blocks per use, and bonemeal may continue even at age 25 until space/build-floor obstruction stops it.

`loot_tables/blocks/dropleaf.json` gives one Dropleaf with shears or Silk Touch. Otherwise it gives one with Fortune table-bonus probabilities 0.33, 0.55, 0.77, and 1.0 at Fortune 0, 1, 2, and 3+; the table has no `survives_explosion` condition. Contrary to a missing-table assumption, `loot_tables/blocks/dropleaf_plant.json` exists but outputs `minecraft:air`, including under its shears/Silk alternative. The stem has no registered item and cannot be acquired directly. Inherited pick/clone on a body should return the head item, but verify.

One trade can bootstrap an effectively unlimited supply: allow at least one body to form, harvest the bottom head with shears, Silk Touch, or Fortune III, and inherited updates leave/convert the lowest supported body to a replacement head that can grow again. This renewal depends on the companion transition and correct bottom-head harvesting, not worldgen.

Both blocks are Fabric-flammable `(60,100)`; only the item-bearing head is compostable at 0.15. No dye, food, fuel, flower/bee, seed, recipe, entity effect, particle, vehicle, weather, or redstone behavior was found. The custom `DropleafFeature` creates ceiling-hung pillars, writes head states with age 23/24, and converts covered earlier heads to body; it is not required for later player growth.

**Runtime boundary.** Confirm initial-age distribution, random/bonemeal counts and build-floor behavior, ceiling predicate, middle/head break conversions, clone item, explosions, and exact renewal. There is no tilt test to preserve beyond explicitly proving that standing/projectiles/redstone do nothing special.

#### `regions_unexplored:barley`

**Confirmed.** Barley is `RuDoublePlantBlock extends DoublePlantBlock`, copying Sunflower properties. It has only `half=lower|upper` (default lower), uses the same block ID in both cells, needs ordinary dirt/farmland support plus a replaceable upper cell, is dry/non-waterloggable, and has no age. The ordinary Barley `BlockItem` places the lower half and inherited `setPlacedBy` adds the upper half. It does not turn into a crop block, does not use separate seeds, has no crop or seed tags, does not random tick, does not implement bonemeal, and cannot spread.

The lower-half-only loot table gives exactly one Barley behind `survives_explosion`; no tool, Silk Touch, Fortune, age, or count branch exists. Hand, shears, hoe, axe, pickaxe, sword, Silk Touch, and Fortune therefore select the same authored output. The upper half has no item entry. Companion removal makes the other half invalid; exact upper-first drop ordering remains a runtime case. A single trade remains finite and selling Barley does **not** bypass a separate seed loop because none exists in this artifact.

Barley is in `c:grains_barley`, `minecraft:replaceable_by_trees`, `minecraft:mineable/axe`, Naturalist's `rhino_charge_breakable`, and RU `replaceable_blocks`; it is compostable at 0.6 and flammable `(60,100)`. Smelting (200 ticks, 0.35 XP) or smoking (100 ticks, 0.35 XP) consumes one Barley and yields one bread. No brewing, other food/crafting, seed, or crop recipe was found. Its block and generated item textures are fixed-color; no color provider is registered.

#### `regions_unexplored:windswept_grass`

**Confirmed.** This is a decorative `RuDoublePlantBlock`, not a crop or growing grass. It copies vanilla tall-grass properties and retains the inherited double-plant dirt/farmland survival rule. It has `half=lower|upper` with default `lower`, needs two cells, is neither waterlogged nor replaceable by another Windswept Grass placement, has no random tick, no bonemeal response, no spread, and no environmental or entity callback.

Its two loot pools cover either targeted half only while the matching companion exists. Shears yield a fixed **two** Windswept Grass items. Without shears, the alternative is one wheat seed at 12.5% behind `survives_explosion`; otherwise nothing. Silk Touch alone does not satisfy the plant-drop condition and Fortune has no function in this table. Consequently the plant itself requires shears in normal survival, but once one traded item is placed, shearing its intact two-cell plant produces two placeable items—a reliable multiplicative supply. Whether breaking either half always yields exactly one two-item stack and suppresses any counterpart loot is runtime-sensitive.

It is directly in common `grass`/`grass_like` block and item tags, `minecraft:replaceable`, `replaceable_by_trees`, `mineable/axe`, and Naturalist's Rhino tag. It is compostable 0.5 and flammable `(60,100)`. `RuColors` supplies biome foliage tint to the placed block and vanilla's fixed default foliage color to the inventory item. No recipe or mob use was found.

#### `regions_unexplored:clover`

**Confirmed.** Clover is `GroundCoverBlock extends BushBlock implements BonemealableBlock`: one horizontally oriented ground-cover cell, not connected horizontal blocks. Its state is `facing` plus `flower_amount=1..4`, default north/1. Using another Clover item on the same cell is allowed below four and increments the amount; placement direction controls a new cell. The outline is a 16×3×16 slab, collision is empty from its properties, and its ordinary dirt/farmland support inherits from `BushBlock`.

Direct bonemeal always reports valid/success. At amounts 1–3 it increments the same cell; at 4 it pops one additional Clover item. It never places a neighboring cell and does not random tick. Breaking returns exactly the stored amount, subject to explosion decay. Thus one purchased item plus bonemeal reaches amount four, after which each successful use yields an item; this is effectively unlimited production at a bone-meal cost. Stacking four purchased items then breaking is only 1:1 storage, not duplication.

Clover is tagged `minecraft:replaceable`, `replaceable_by_trees`, `mineable/hoe`, Naturalist Rhino-breakable, and RU replaceable; it is not in the flower/bee tags and has no dye, stew, food, or other recipe/effect. It is compostable 0.2 and flammable `(60,100)`. The placed block uses grass-biome tint; the item uses vanilla's fixed default grass color, so a trade screen should be stable but not biome-derived.

#### `regions_unexplored:grass_sprouts` and the historical identity

**Confirmed absence.** The exact JAR contains no `grass_sprouts` ID, display name, item, class, model, texture, tag, loot table, recipe, or feature and no `GrassSproutsBlock`. It therefore cannot establish current substrate rules, soil-and-stone support, tint, drops, shears/seeds, generation, renewability, or trade placement for Grass Sprouts. The requested merged-descendant relationship is not proven by this artifact.

The artifact does prove the older concepts are separate:

- `stone_bud` is a `RuStonePlantBlock` whose sole support predicate is `#regions_unexplored:stone_plant_can_survive_on`. That tag includes `#minecraft:terracotta`, `#minecraft:base_stone_overworld`, `#minecraft:base_stone_nether`, `#c:ores`, calcite, chalk/chalk grass, dripstone block, gravel, Ash/Volcanic Ash, `#c:gravel`, `#c:stone`, gilded blackstone, mossy stone, Argillite, Viridescent/Deepslate Viridescent Nylium, Deepslate/Stone/Argillite grass. It does not accept normal soil merely through `#minecraft:dirt`. It is not bonemealable, uses grass tint for block and item, and drops itself only with shears; otherwise it has a 12.5% wheat-seed branch with Fortune bonus.
- `medium_grass` is `RuPlantBlock` on ordinary dirt/farmland. It uses grass tint, is bonemealable, and bonemeal replaces it with vanilla **short grass** rather than producing another Medium Grass. Its loot is itself with shears or a 12.5% Fortune-affected wheat-seed branch.
- `redstone_bud` is a separately registered `RedstoneBudBlock`, with its own assets, loot, recipe, advancement, configured/placed feature, and redstone-cave biomes. It is explicitly not part of this audit.

**Version-bounded lineage proof, not substituted behavior.** Official upstream commit [`e6d3e7c4160fc1fe73cb8eeaa0ebd47b8fbb4493`](https://github.com/UHQ-GAMES-MODS/RegionsUnexplored/commit/e6d3e7c4160fc1fe73cb8eeaa0ebd47b8fbb4493) (2026-03-07, source version `0.6+indev`, Minecraft `1.21.1`) explicitly says “merge Medium Grass and Stone Bud into Grass Sprouts.” Its diff removes both predecessor registrations, adds `GRASS_SPROUTS` using `GrassSproutsBlock`, renames Stone Bud assets, and replaces both color entries. This confirms the historical descendant claim, but the commit is newer than and not tied to the exact JAR, so its mechanics are planning context only; the exact-artifact trade classification remains `UNRESOLVED`.

In that later source only, `GrassSproutsBlock extends BushBlock implements BonemealableBlock`; it has one property-free/default state, shape `(2,0,2)..(14,5,14)`, and survives on `#regions_unexplored:supports_grass_sprouts`. The generated tag contains `#minecraft:supports_vegetation`, `#minecraft:terracotta`, `#minecraft:base_stone_overworld`, `#minecraft:base_stone_nether`, `minecraft:calcite`, optional `#c:ores`, RU Chalk and Chalk Grass Block, `minecraft:dripstone_block`, optional `#c:gravel` and `#c:stone`, `minecraft:gilded_blackstone`, RU Mossy Stone, Argillite, Viridescent Nylium, Deepslate Viridescent Nylium, and Deepslate/Stone/Argillite Grass Blocks. The old explicit RU Ash and Volcanic Ash entries are absent; optional common-tag expansion is runtime/datapack-dependent. Thus it is still meaningfully stone-associated **and** soil/vegetation-associated. Bonemeal converts it to vanilla short grass. Its later tinted-cross model and color registration use biome grass tint in world and a fixed grass item tint. Its later loot gives itself only with shears; otherwise it offers a 12.5% Fortune-boosted wheat-seed branch. One item is finite: shearing preserves 1:1, seeds do not grow Grass Sprouts, and bonemeal consumes its identity.

Later direct worldgen uses `patch/grass_sprouts` through sparse and dense placed features across the former Medium Grass/Stone Bud biome sets, plus mixed providers `joshua_desert → patch/grass_sparse` (short grass + Grass Sprouts) and `redwoods`/`sparse_redwoods → patch/ferns_dense` (ferns, short grass, Grass Sprouts). Those paths would require direct feature removal plus state-level pruning of the mixed providers if, and only if, later merged semantics are selected for the 26.2 port. They are not claimed as paths in the exact 0.5.6 artifact.

Restoring a historical stone-only identity from that later merged block would require a behavior change: narrow the support predicate or implement a separate block, and decide its loot/tint/worldgen/alias contract. It is not achieved by using later Grass Sprouts unchanged. A later observed alias maps `medium_grass` to `grass_sprouts`, while a second alias spells `stone` rather than `stone_bud`; that possible upstream typo/datafix gap is also unresolved for any future migration.

#### `regions_unexplored:mycotoxic_daisy`

**Confirmed.** Mycotoxic Daisy is not a `FlowerBlock`; it is `RuNetherDoublePlantBlock extends DoublePlantBlock`. Its state is only `half=lower|upper` (default lower). An ordinary same-ID `BlockItem` places a complete two-cell plant through vanilla double-plant placement. The lower half accepts ordinary dirt/farmland, `#minecraft:nylium`, or soul soil; the artifact adds Brimsprout, Cobalt, Glistering and Viridescent variants, Overgrown Bone Block, and the block registered as `mycotoxic_moss` to relevant support tags. The `potted_mycotoxic_daisy` loot table gives a pot plus `minecraft:air`, not the contained plant; ordinary Flower Pot right-click extraction uses different code and needs a separate test. #### `regions_unexplored:cattail`

**Confirmed.** `CattailBlock extends DoublePlantBlock implements SimpleWaterloggedBlock`. One ordinary same-ID item places a lower and upper state; properties are `half=lower|upper` and `waterlogged=true|false`, default lower/false. There is no separate stem/item. Placement requires a replaceable upper cell and support in `#regions_unexplored:cattail_can_survive_on`: `#minecraft:dirt`, `#minecraft:sand`, clay, or gravel. This includes mud through the dirt tag and ordinary/custom dirt expansions; sand tag expansion is datapack-sensitive. Vanilla farmland is not separately accepted (RU Peat/Silt Farmland happen to be accepted because this JAR adds them to `#minecraft:dirt`).

The lower cell may be air/dry or a water fluid and records its target fluid as `waterlogged`; the upper placed state is dry. Its support predicate also rejects water in the would-be **upper cell**, so a fully submerged two-block water column cannot host it initially. This permits dry placement or a one-block-deep waterlogged lower half with a dry upper; a shoreline is not required. Both halves expose `waterlogged`, so bucket-adding water to the upper **after** placement may create an invalid-but-persistent edge until a survival-relevant update—do not infer that post-placement upper water is impossible. Removing lower water should leave a valid dry plant. Waterlogged neighbor updates schedule a water fluid tick. It cannot be replaced by another item in place.

There is no bonemeal, random growth, vertical extension, spreading, or duplication: maximum height is exactly two. Lower-half-only loot yields one Cattail behind `survives_explosion`; no tool/enchantment branch exists. Static `DoublePlantBlock.playerWillDestroy` behavior predicts lower break → one item and upper removal, upper break → zero item and lower removal; neighbor/support invalidation is predicted to remove without block loot. Fluid restoration and exact update order require runtime.

The block is no-collision/cutout and entities pass through. No mob, projectile, vehicle, damage, effect, redstone, particle, or special-use code exists. It is flammable `(60,100)`, compostable 0.6, and tagged `mineable/axe`, `replaceable_by_trees`, Naturalist Rhino-breakable, and RU replaceable. Crafting gives one brown dye; Create milling gives two brown dye. It has fixed-color assets and no tint provider.

#### `regions_unexplored:duckweed`

**Confirmed.** `DuckweedBlock extends WaterlilyBlock`; it declares no properties and therefore has one default block state. `BlockRegistry.registerPlaceOnWaterBlock` pairs it with a vanilla `PlaceOnWaterBlockItem`, so the standard item raycasts a water surface and places the exact Duckweed block in the air cell above it. It does not replace the water block and has no `waterlogged` state: the source/support remains below. The block survives when the support fluid is water **or** the support block is any `IceBlock`, provided the fluid in its own cell is empty. It therefore floats “on” water/ice, not “in” water.

It retains the Waterlily outline `(1,0,1)..(15,1.5,15)` but has no collision and explicitly overrides `entityInside` to do nothing, removing vanilla Lily Pad's boat-destruction callback. It propagates skylight, has no state, and is replaceable by a different held item but not by another Duckweed item. Losing/changing the water-or-ice support invalidates it. Flowing water satisfies the static survival test, while the specialized open-water raycast is source-only; edge-assisted placement above flow is unresolved. Regular Ice and Frosted Ice are `IceBlock`-family supports; Packed/Blue Ice should not be assumed without runtime/class confirmation.

Duckweed does not random tick, bonemeal, or spread across a surface. Its loot is one Duckweed behind `survives_explosion`, with no tool or enchantment condition; no shears/Silk/Fortune behavior exists. It is compostable 0.15, flammable `(60,100)`, and appears in `inside_step_sound_blocks`, `mineable/axe`, and RU replaceable. There is no dye, food, stew, fuel, or crafting recipe. Its model has a tint index but `RuColors` registers no Duckweed provider and its texture carries color; final block/item rendering should be checked rather than assuming water tint.

#### `regions_unexplored:tassel`

**Confirmed.** Tassel is `RuDoubleFlowerBlock extends DoublePlantBlock implements BonemealableBlock`, copies Sunflower properties, uses one ID and `half=lower|upper` with default `lower`, needs ordinary dirt/farmland and two cells, and does not random tick or spread. It is a tall flower/multipart plant, not grass or a crop.

The crucial override is `use`: when the player holds bone meal, it calls `Block.popResource` for one item of the clicked Tassel block and returns mapped `InteractionResult.PASS` (`class_1269.field_5811`), not `SUCCESS`. Its formal `isValidBonemealTarget` and `isBonemealSuccess` return true while `performBonemeal` is empty. Static interaction dispatch therefore predicts that block use first ejects a Tassel, then PASS allows the Bone Meal item's successful use path to consume one bone meal even though the empty callback changes no plant state. The predicted loop is one new Tassel per consumed bone meal, from either half, indefinitely. A dispenser calls the bonemeal interface without player block use, so it is predicted to consume bone meal and produce **no** Tassel. Runtime must confirm dispatch, crouch-use, sided spawning, automation, and consumption.

The lower-only loot table gives one item behind `survives_explosion` with any tool; the upper has no item entry. Tassel is in Minecraft/common tall-flower/flower block and item tags, is bee-attractive, compostable 0.6, flammable `(60,100)`, and tagged `mineable/axe`, `replaceable_by_trees`, and RU replaceable. Crafting gives one light-gray dye. Create milling instead gives two **white** dye. The two-layer block model tints stems/crosses with biome grass color but leaves the flower overlay fixed; `RuColors` registers only a block provider. Its generated inventory texture is fixed, so trade-screen rendering should be stable while placed color varies by biome.

## 7. Future worldgen-suppression analysis

No plant-specific upstream configuration was found. RU's biome toggles are much broader than the requested policy and would remove whole biomes, so they are not recommended. The clean future 26.2 design should simply omit natural-acquisition registrations when the plants are reconstructed. If compatibility against a complete RU port is instead required, use the narrow targets below.

For mixed `weighted_state_provider`, `noise_provider`, or `simple_random_selector` resources, an exact datapack override can retain non-target entries, but it must be maintained against the exact target version. Where an individual configured provider cannot be made empty validly, remove its biome/placed reference or use a narrowly keyed compatibility hook rather than replacing it with an unrelated plant. A mixin is warranted only if 26.2 APIs offer no data-driven per-reference/provider override.

Flower-provider frequency deserves special handling: neutralizing only outer placement modifiers may stop chunk decoration while vanilla grass bonemeal still discovers and invokes the configured `flower` feature. To enforce “Ribbit is the first source,” tests must prove both fresh-chunk absence and grass-bonemeal absence. Removing target states from providers closes both. This does not remove specimen-driven propagation because Hyssop/Lupines have none. Clover, Windswept Grass, Dropleaf and Tassel renewability lives in block/loot behavior and remains intact when their placed-feature paths are suppressed.

All suppression methods affect newly generated chunks and later feature/bonemeal calls. Already generated plants and player inventories remain. A future clean gate therefore needs a fresh world/uncharted-area expectation or a separate, explicitly authorized migration decision. Feature suppression alone never removes block loot, recipes, or already-held items; those need no removal here because they do not create a first specimen from nothing, except the explicitly identified biome grass-bonemeal paths.

## 11. Uncertainties and confidence limits

- The supplied artifact's own metadata says 0.5.6 for Minecraft 1.20.1-era APIs, contradicting the request's “1.21.1-era” label. Grass Sprouts is absent. The later official merge commit proves lineage but is not exact-artifact behavior; the eventual 26.2 target must deliberately choose an implementation authority.
- Decompilation can obscure generic names and compiler bridges. Every intermediary class/method used for a material conclusion was cross-checked against the pinned 1.20.1 mappings or resource behavior, but the result remains static evidence.
- Minecraft 26.2 superclass, item-dispatch, registry, tags, feature codecs, loot semantics, color APIs, and renderer behavior may differ. No statement here is a claim that the old implementation will compile or behave unchanged.
- Dropleaf's exact initial age distribution, stochastic bonemeal count, random-tick frequency as observed, minimum/maximum realized height, ceiling-face edge cases, build-floor stop, head/body conversion after middle or head breaks, explosion behavior, clone item, and renewable harvest loop require runtime confirmation. It has no Big Dripleaf tilt behavior; a negative entity/projectile/redstone test should confirm that absence.
- For every same-ID double plant, the lower-only loot plus inherited companion cleanup predicts the stated item count, but upper-first, lower-first, support-loss, creative, explosion, and orphan-state sequences must be observed. This is especially important for Windswept Grass's paired two-item pools and Cattail fluid restoration.
- Authored potted loot definitively names `minecraft:air` instead of the contained plant for Mycotoxic Daisy, Hyssop, Lupines, and Meadow Sage. Flower Pot right-click removal uses a different inherited path, so “break loses it” and “right-click retrieves it” are separate runtime cases.
- Vanilla grass-bonemeal bytecode statically selects the biome's first configured flower feature, but 26.2 feature ordering and any compatibility injections can change which provider wins. Bamboo/Willow negative routes and every suppression profile need runtime sampling.
- Suspicious-stew effects follow 1.20.1 `FlowerBlock` and tag semantics; verify Hyssop Luck duration and Lupine Saturation presentation/instant semantics in the target stack. Bee attraction likewise depends on target-version tag consumers.
- Mycotoxic Daisy has no toxic callback in the artifact. Cross-mod event hooks could still react to its tags/ID at runtime; the audit cannot prove the absence of behavior injected by mods not contained in the JAR.
- Cattail's class proves lower waterlogging, initial-placement upper-cell water rejection, and fluid tick scheduling. Exact bucket pickup/fill permissions, placement into source versus flowing water, survival after drainage, and which water source is restored when either half breaks remain runtime questions.
- Duckweed's survival predicate accepts any water fluid state or IceBlock, while the specialized item raycast and ordinary `useOn` paths determine what a player can target. Source, flowing water, ice, water removal/flow, replacement by another item, boats, and rendering require live confirmation.
- Tassel's mapped result is `PASS`, and vanilla dispatch predicts a spawned item followed by one consumed bone meal. Confirm client/server spawning, whether either half behaves identically, and that no double spawn or free use occurs.
- Meadow Sage's item is both food and BlockItem. Placement versus eating priority, hunger gating, the 50% Instant Health effect, potted behavior, and target-version component representation must be tested.
- Texture/model inspection distinguishes registered tint providers from fixed art; final perceived colors, especially Duckweed's unused block tint index and Tassel's layered tint, require rendering in a trade UI and multiple biomes.
- Disabling features changes new chunks, not existing chunks. The practical cleanup/migration policy for an established world is outside this audit.
