## Current visual checks

1. With the exact recorded candidate, Matcha Flavoured, Trinkets, Inventory Extended, and the accepted Trinkets × Inventory Extended coordinate contract active, open Survival Inventory at representative GUI scales. Confirm every Trinkets frame, group background, empty-slot icon, item, hover state, and highlight belongs visually to the Matcha inventory without moving its click target.
2. Repeat in Creative → Survival Inventory. Confirm the six Inventory Extended rows, hotbar, player model, controls, and every attached or generic Trinkets wrapper remain aligned and unobstructed.
3. Exercise empty and equipped belt, charm, necklace, and any dynamically contributed groups. Confirm each logical slot has exactly one frame and item, the intended empty icon resolves, and no fallback or detached duplicate appears.
4. Equip and unequip representative items through ordinary interaction. Confirm visual styling does not alter slot identity, equip eligibility, quick-move behavior, item ownership, or Inventory Extended mapping.
5. Change GUI scale, reload resources, save and reload, then leave and rejoin once. Confirm the presentation remains stable with no clipping into the player model, recipe book, controls, or adjacent inventory areas.
6. Inspect the matching client log for missing sprites, GUI/resource exceptions, Mixin failures, menu or slot-index errors, and project-owned warnings. Attribute whole-screen Matcha × Inventory Extended composition defects and logical Trinkets × Inventory Extended defects to their independent owners.

## Stop conditions

Stop and preserve the exact candidate, dependency, screenshot, and log evidence on any moved or mismatched click target, duplicate or detached slot/item, missing or magenta/black sprite, clipped control or player model, changed equip or quick-move behavior, incorrect Inventory Extended mapping, client/server disagreement, crash, or relevant GUI/resource exception. Do not patch Trinkets or Inventory Extended production logic, start a new Trinkets API port, or promote an artifact as part of visual diagnosis.
